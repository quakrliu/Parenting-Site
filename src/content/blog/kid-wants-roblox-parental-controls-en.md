**TL;DR:** Roblox rolled out mandatory facial age verification for chat access in January 2026, then split accounts into Roblox Kids (ages 5-8) and Roblox Select (ages 9-15), announced in April and rolled out globally in June 2026. Minecraft's safety runs through the separate Microsoft Family Safety app, not the game itself. Neither one is safe "out of the box." Before letting your kid download anything, set aside about 40 minutes to audit account settings, link your own parent account, cap the Robux allowance, and turn off default chat. Below is the exact list, plus what the research actually says about how much game time is reasonable.

Maybe your kid came home from a friend's birthday party and did not take their shoes off before asking, "Can I get Roblox?" Apparently a few kids at the party had it open on a shared iPad the whole afternoon, building some kind of pizza restaurant, and your kid spent two hours watching over shoulders instead of playing. They are not asking permission so much as informing you of a decision that has already been made by consensus at the party.

"Let me look into it" is a perfectly good answer — it's parent code for "I need forty minutes and a strong coffee before I can answer this honestly." At BloomPath, half of what I build is about giving parents a clearer picture of what's actually happening with a kid's development, so my first instinct with something like this isn't to panic — it's to go read the actual settings menu like it's a codebase I've inherited from someone else.

## Is Roblox Actually Safe for Kids in 2026?

Not by default, but it's meaningfully safer than it was two years ago. As of January 2026, Roblox requires facial age estimation for any user who wants chat access — a short selfie-style scan processed by a third-party verification provider that sorts kids into age bands and limits who they can talk to. Roughly 45% of U.S. kids under 13 play Roblox at least weekly, so this wasn't a niche fix; it was Roblox responding to years of reporting about adults contacting children inside game experiences.

Here's the pattern safety researchers keep flagging, and it's worth saying plainly instead of vaguely: an adult approaches a child inside a game, compliments their build or their outfit, sends a small gift of Robux, and after a few days of friendly chat suggests moving the conversation to Discord, Snapchat, or Telegram — platforms with far less oversight than Roblox's own chat. The age verification step is meant to slow that down, not eliminate it. Reports earlier this year found some users bypassing the checks with edited photos, and the AI estimator has misjudged ages in both directions. Treat this as an improved system, not a solved problem.

## What Are Roblox's 2026 Parental Controls, Actually?

Roblox now separates accounts by age band, and the controls differ meaningfully between them. Kids ages 5-8 get a **Roblox Kids** account with chat disabled by default. Ages 9-15 get **Roblox Select**, which allows more communication but keeps guardrails in place. The feature that matters most is **Linked Parent Accounts** — connecting your own Roblox login to your kid's lets you see their friends list, set a monthly Robux allowance instead of an open-ended spending permission, and review a "Parental Insights" dashboard that shows who they've been chatting with.

None of this turns on by itself. You have to go looking for it in account settings, and the menu labeling isn't especially intuitive — I clicked through three different settings screens before I found the linked-account option, which tells you something about how much Roblox is still prioritizing account growth over making safety the obvious default path.

## How Do I Set Up Minecraft's Parental Controls?

This one surprised me. Minecraft doesn't have its own built-in parental dashboard — safety is managed entirely through the separate **Microsoft Family Safety** app, which you set up outside the game itself. The single most important thing that app does is block "unvetted" multiplayer servers, meaning random servers run by strangers that your kid could otherwise join with a few taps. If your kid plays Minecraft without Family Safety configured, they can join any public server with open chat, no age gate, no vetting at all. I'd rank this as a bigger blind spot than Roblox right now, precisely because it's less talked about.

## What's a Reasonable Amount of Video Game Time?

The commonly cited range is 1-2 hours on school days and up to 3 hours on weekends, and average teen screen use is sitting around 2.8 hours a day. But I want to push back gently on treating that number as the whole answer, because a clock doesn't tell you anything about what's happening during that time. Thirty minutes of my daughter building something collaboratively with a friend she already knows from school is a completely different experience than thirty minutes in an open chat lobby with strangers, even though both would show up identically on a screen time app.

Mei's take on this, from the parenting-methods side of things, is that the question "how long" usually matters less than "with whom" and "doing what" — which lines up with the newer [5C framework guidance](/en/blog/aap-5cs-screen-time-framework-preschoolers-en) we've written about before: content, context, calm, communication, and consistency, rather than a hard stopwatch number.

## The 5-Step Setup to Run Before Saying Yes

Here's the setup, in order:

1. **Create a linked parent account first.** Make your own Roblox login before your kid makes theirs, so their account is born already connected to yours, not retrofitted later.
2. **Set the account to the correct age band and verify it stuck.** Double-check that the account actually landed in the Roblox Kids or Roblox Select tier that matches your kid's age — the age estimation step doesn't always run cleanly, so confirm it manually rather than assume it worked.
3. **Turn off open chat and set a Robux allowance cap.** A fixed monthly amount instead of open spending, and chat turned off with anyone outside their existing friends-of-friends network.
4. **Put the device in the living room, not their room.** This is the least technical step and honestly the most effective one. Nothing replaces being able to glance over from the kitchen table.
5. **Schedule a recurring monthly check.** Not a one-time setup — a calendar reminder to reopen the Parental Insights dashboard and look at who your kid has actually been talking to, because settings drift and friend requests pile up.

It's tempting to skip step 5. It can feel like overkill for a kid who just wants to build a pizza restaurant. But the gift-then-migrate-to-Discord pattern is exactly why the reminder belongs in your calendar anyway.

## What If My Kid Already Has an Account I Didn't Set Up?

If this is you — and it's a lot of parents, since kids often get accounts at a friend's house or on a hand-me-down tablet before you're even in the loop — the audit is the same five steps, just run in reverse. Log in with your kid present (not behind their back; this works better as a "let's do this together" conversation than a surprise inspection), check the current age band setting, review the existing friends list for anyone they don't actually know in person, and reset the chat permissions from scratch. It takes about the same 40 minutes either way.

Years into parenting, I still catch myself wanting a single toggle that makes all of this safe permanently. There isn't one. Settings drift, platforms update their policies, and the conversation has to happen more than once. That's not a failure of the tools — it's just what raising a kid in 2026 actually involves.

## FAQ

**Is Roblox appropriate for a 6-year-old?**
Roblox Kids accounts (ages 5-8) exist specifically for this age range and disable chat by default, which is the setting that matters most for younger kids. Even with that account type, keep play in a shared space rather than a bedroom.

**Does Minecraft need the same level of setup as Roblox?**
Yes, arguably more, because Minecraft's safety controls live entirely outside the game in the Microsoft Family Safety app. Without configuring it, your kid can join open multiplayer servers with no vetting at all.

**How much Robux allowance is reasonable?**
There's no universal number, but the point of the Linked Parent Accounts feature is to replace open-ended spending with a fixed monthly cap you set yourself, so a surprise $60 in-game purchase can't happen on a card you forgot was linked.

**What's the biggest red flag in a kid's chat history?**
Any adult stranger who has sent gifts (in-game currency, items) and then suggested continuing the conversation on a different app like Discord or Snapchat. This is a documented grooming pattern, not a one-off coincidence.

**Should I just say no to video games altogether?**
You can, but given that roughly 45% of kids under 13 already play Roblox weekly, an outright ban often just means your kid plays unsupervised at a friend's house instead, where you have zero visibility into settings. Setting it up carefully on your own terms usually beats banning it entirely.
